Patio coatings in Chula Vista give tired outdoor concrete a clean, textured finish that stands up to year-round sun. We coat back patios, front entries, walkways and side yards with polyaspartic systems, which stay color-stable outdoors where standard epoxy would yellow.

What Does a Patio Coating Involve?

The slab is cleaned and diamond-ground to remove dirt, old paint and sealers. Cracks are repaired, and saw-cut control joints are kept open so the slab can still move with the seasons.

A polyaspartic base coat goes down with a broadcast of flakes in sand, stone or gray tones. A clear polyaspartic topcoat with anti-slip grit seals it. Most patios are done in a day or two.

When Do You Need a Patio Coating?

People call when deck paint is peeling, when rust, fertilizer or barbecue stains won’t come out, or when an old slab looks worn next to new landscaping. A coating is also a good finishing step before adding an outdoor kitchen or new patio furniture.

Landlords and sellers often coat patios and entries as a quick way to freshen a property before photos.

Why Do Outdoor Coatings Fail?

The biggest reason is sunlight. Standard epoxy breaks down under UV light, turning amber and chalky, which is why it belongs indoors. Thin patio paints sit on top of the concrete and peel once water gets under them.

Moisture is the other cause. Many patios were poured without a vapor barrier, so ground moisture moves up through the slab. A breathable approach and proper grinding keep the coating attached.

What Affects the Cost of a Patio Coating in Chula Vista?

  • Patio size — the coated area, including walkways and entries
  • Old paint — deck paint or sealer that has to be ground off
  • Cracks and joints — repairs and how the control joints are handled
  • Steps and edges — risers, step noses and exposed slab edges
  • Flake blend — the color and size of flakes chosen
  • Texture — how much anti-slip grit the space needs

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a coated patio get hot in the sun?

Any surface in direct sun warms up, and darker colors get hotter than lighter ones. Light sand, beige and stone flake blends stay noticeably cooler underfoot, so those are the usual picks for open patios.

Will a patio coating be slippery when wet?

A flake coating has natural texture, and an anti-slip grit is added to the topcoat on outdoor floors. That gives better footing when sprinklers or rain wet the surface.

How soon can we use the patio after it's coated?

Polyaspartic cures quickly, so most patios are ready for foot traffic the next day. Furniture and grills can usually go back a day or two after that.

Why not use regular epoxy on a patio?

Standard epoxy is not UV-stable. In direct sun it turns yellow or amber and can get a chalky surface within months. Polyaspartic keeps its color, which is why it is the go-to for outdoor concrete.
